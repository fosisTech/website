# Ramadan at University: A Complete Guide for Students and ISoc Leaders

From managing fasts during exam season to running suhoor events that actually work — everything you need to know about making Ramadan on campus truly meaningful.

Ramadan at university is a unique experience. It is also, for many students, one of the most spiritually significant months of their degree. The right support and environment can make it transformative. The wrong one can make it lonely and exhausting. This guide is for both students navigating it personally, and ISoc leaders who want to create something special for their community.

## For Students: Managing the Fast

The practical challenges of Ramadan at university are real. Late Taraweeh prayers conflict with early morning lectures. Suhoor requires waking in the small hours in noisy accommodation. Long summer fasts, when Ramadan falls in exam season, demand careful energy management. Here is what works:

**Plan your sleep in advance.** The single biggest factor in surviving a Ramadan fast while studying is sleep. Work backwards from your Fajr time to determine when you need to be in bed. Treat this like a timetable commitment.

**Talk to your tutors early.** Most universities now have guidance on supporting Muslim students during Ramadan. If you have a particularly demanding schedule, a five-minute conversation with your personal tutor at the start of term can prevent a lot of stress later. You are not asking for special treatment, you are communicating your needs, which is a professional skill.

**Find your people.** Ramadan fasted alone is much harder than Ramadan shared. Your ISoc is the obvious place, but also look for the Muslim students in your department, on your corridor, in your sports team. Breaking fast together changes everything.

## For ISoc Leaders: Creating the Environment

The ISocs that make Ramadan special are not the ones with the biggest budgets. They are the ones that are intentional about what they are creating. A few principles:

**Start planning in Sha’ban.** Ramadan programming that is planned two weeks before Ramadan shows. The logistics of regular Iftaar events (venues, catering, speakers, volunteers) need at least six weeks of lead time to do well.

**Iftaar is not the whole programme.** The best ISoc Ramadans include morning circles before Fajr, mid-afternoon Quran sessions during the energy dip, and a consistent space for reflection after Taraweeh. Iftaar is the highlight, not the entirety.

**Include everyone.** Ramadan is an opportunity for your ISoc to welcome non-Muslim students into something genuinely beautiful. Interfaith Iftaar events, if handled with care and intention, are among the most powerful things an ISoc can do for its university community. Do not squander the opportunity.

**Look out for students who are struggling.** Not everyone has an easy Ramadan. Mental health challenges, family difficulties, spiritual doubts, these are all real. Make sure your committee knows how to check in, how to signpost support, and that no one has to navigate difficulty alone.

## Resources from FOSIS

Every year FOSIS produces a Ramadan Resource Pack for ISocs, including prayer timetable templates, Iftaar planning guides, speaker booking support, and welfare signposting materials. ISocs affiliated with FOSIS receive these automatically. Non-affiliated ISocs can access them via our [ISoc Essentials page](https://fosis.org.uk/isoc-essentials/).

Ramadan Mubarak to every Muslim student and ISoc leader across the UK and Ireland. We see the work you do. It matters more than you know.
