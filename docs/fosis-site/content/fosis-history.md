The Beginning

1963

## A Meeting that Changed Muslim Student Life in Britain

In July 1963, representatives from eleven Islamic societies gathered in the Cadbury Room at the University of Birmingham. What they agreed to that day would shape Muslim student life in the UK and Ireland for generations.

The need was clear: Muslim students across British and Irish universities were isolated, lacking a unified voice and the resources to practise their faith on campus and represent their interests nationally. Individual Islamic societies existed, but they operated alone. A national body was long overdue.

At that meeting, the Federation of Islamic Student Societies, FOISS, was formally established, later renamed the Federation of Student Islamic Societies (FOSIS). The organisation was built on a conviction that Muslim students, united, could be a force for good on every campus in the country.

In the early 1960s, FOSIS was the only support group that many young Muslims in the UK and Ireland could look to for support and advice. The eleven founding societies represented the entirety of organised Muslim student life in Britain at the time.

Six Decades of History

## The FOSIS Timeline

Pre-FOSIS

1960

Muslim Students Society UK & Eire Founded

The Muslim Students Society of UK & Eire (MSS) is established, one of the direct precursors to FOSIS, and the first organised attempt to unite Muslim students across British universities. Dr Ahmad Totonji, a student from northern Iraq studying engineering in Britain, is among its co-founders.

1961

United Muslim Student Organisation of Europe

UMSO is established, deepening the networks between Muslim students across Europe and laying further groundwork for a UK national body. Totonji is again a founding contributor, developing a vision for interconnected Muslim student movements across borders.

January 1963

1963

MSA Founded in Illinois, Totonji Crosses the Atlantic

In January 1963, Ahmad Totonji, alongside seventy other Muslim students, assembles at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign and co-founds the Muslim Students Association (MSA) of the United States and Canada. He travels to the US to pursue a PhD in Petroleum Engineering, but leaves behind the organisational foundations that will feed directly into FOSIS six months later.

◆ Founding, July 1963

FOSIS is Born, the Cadbury Room, Birmingham

July 1963: eleven Islamic societies convene at the University of Birmingham and formally establish the Federation of Islamic Student Societies (FOISS), renamed FOSIS later that year. The organisation immediately becomes the only national support group for Muslim students across the UK and Ireland.

Historic, November–December 1964

1964

Malcolm X’s British Tour, Organised by FOSIS

Just one year after its founding, FOSIS organises a landmark UK speaking tour for El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz (Malcolm X), following his transformative Hajj. FOSIS General Secretary Ebrahimsa Mohamed leads the organisation, alongside Abdul Wahid Hamid, Hoossain Rajah, and Ghayasuddin Siddiqui. The detailed itinerary: **22 Nov**, first speech at Malaysia Hall, Marble Arch, London; **1 Dec**, formal FOSIS reception, London; **3 Dec**, Oxford Union debate; **4 Dec**, Manchester and Sheffield; **5 Dec**, Islamic Cultural Centre, Regent’s Park, and second Malaysia Hall address. Malcolm X was assassinated just ten weeks later, on 21 February 1965.

1965–67

Early Leadership, Oyekan and Mohamed

Dr Oyekan serves as FOSIS General Secretary in 1965 and is elected President in 1967. He goes on to chair The Muslim Editorial Board 1968–70. Ebrahimsa Mohamed is elected President for 1967–68, beginning a decade of institution-building that would culminate in FOSIS’s first permanent home.

1970s

1972

FOSIS Acquires its Permanent Home, 38 Mapesbury Road, Kilburn

With the help of Salem Azzam, Counsellor at the Saudi Arabian Embassy, FOSIS secures funding to purchase a residential property at 38 Mapesbury Road, Kilburn, London NW2, in 1971. Converted into a Muslim student hostel and national head office, it opens formally in November 1972. With Ebrahimsa Mohamed as warden-in-residence, Mapesbury Road rapidly becomes a hub of Muslim community life, hosting wedding ceremonies, conversion ceremonies, and receptions for newly arrived students from across the world. It remains FOSIS’s registered address to this day.

1970s

A Generation of Scholars Passes Through

FOSIS produces alumni who go on to shape British Muslim intellectual life for decades. Among them: Prof Salim T. S. Al-Hassani, who later becomes Emeritus Professor of Mechanical Engineering at Manchester, founds the Foundation for Science, Technology and Civilisation (FSTC), and edits the landmark “1001 Inventions: Muslim Heritage in Our World”, published by National Geographic with a foreword by HRH Prince Charles.

1980s

The Winter Gathering, Hosting the World’s Scholars

FOSIS’s Annual Winter Gathering becomes a fixture in the British Muslim calendar, drawing some of the world’s foremost Islamic scholars. Sheikh Ahmed Deedat addresses the 23rd Annual Winter Gathering, encouraging students to engage in dawah. Dr Khurram Murad, leading thinker of the Islamic Foundation, Leicester, and Imam Siraj Wahhaj of the USA also address FOSIS gatherings, cementing FOSIS’s role as a bridge between global Islamic scholarship and British campus life.

International, 1995–1996

Co-Founding FEMYSO, Europe’s Muslim Youth Voice

The first pan-European meeting of Muslim youth organisations takes place in Sweden in 1995, under the ‘Islam in Europe’ conference. In June 1996, FOSIS co-hosts the formal founding meeting in Leicester. Thirty-five participants from 19 organisations across 11 European countries attend, officially launching FEMYSO, the Forum of European Muslim Youth and Student Organisations. FOSIS is a founding member. FEMYSO now represents 32 member organisations across 22 countries and is headquartered in Brussels.

2000s

Othman Moqbel Leads FOSIS

Othman Moqbel serves as FOSIS President from 2001 to 2004. He goes on to become CEO of Human Appeal (2010–18), growing it into one of the UK’s fastest-expanding charities, before becoming CEO of Action for Humanity, the parent of Syria Relief, the largest Syria-focused NGO in the UK. One of the clearest examples of FOSIS alumni going on to build transformative humanitarian institutions.

7 July 2005

7/7, FOSIS Speaks for Muslim Students

On the day of the London bombings, FOSIS President Wakkas Khan issues an immediate public statement condemning the attacks and expressing the shock and distress of Muslim students across the country. FOSIS maintains a strong media presence in the aftermath, offering Muslim student perspectives and standing against both terrorism and the Islamophobia that followed. From 7/7 onwards, FOSIS becomes a constant voice defending Muslim students under attack in the press and in public discourse.

50th Anniversary, 2013

FOSIS Celebrates Half a Century

FOSIS marks fifty years of service with its 50th Annual Conference, the largest annual gathering of Muslim students in the UK. The conference brings together the next generation of Muslim leaders under one roof for three days of discussion, debate, and community. Delegates reflect on six decades of advocacy, scholarship, and service, from the Cadbury Room to campuses across the nation.

2015

#StudentNotSuspect, FOSIS Takes on Prevent

When the Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015 imposes the Prevent Duty on universities, FOSIS leads the national student response. Emergency motions are submitted to Student Unions at LSE, UCL, SOAS, Queen Mary’s, Cardiff, and Kingston, urging them to oppose the bill. FOSIS launches #StudentNotSuspect, arguing that Prevent disproportionately targets and stigmatises Muslim students, treating them as suspects rather than scholars. It becomes one of the most significant civil liberties campaigns in the history of British higher education.

Historic First, 2016

First Female President in 53 Years

Zara Mohammed, a Glasgow-based LLM graduate in Human Rights Law who had served on the FOSIS Executive Team for three years, is elected President for 2016–17, becoming the first woman to lead FOSIS in its 53-year history. Her election signals a new era of inclusion for Muslim women in student leadership. She goes on to serve as MCB Assistant Secretary General (2018–20), before becoming the youngest and first female Secretary General of the Muslim Council of Britain in January 2021.

2020

FOSIS Foundation Incorporated

The FOSIS Foundation is formally incorporated as a legal entity on 26 October 2020 (Companies House no. 12974947), registered at 38 Mapesbury Road, London NW2 4JD, the same address as the hostel established nearly fifty years earlier. The Foundation formalises FOSIS’s institutional infrastructure for the next chapter of its history.

Present

350,000 Students. One Voice.

FOSIS now represents over 350,000 Muslim university students across the UK and Ireland, operating through 7 regional committees (Scotland, Ireland, North, Wales & West, Midlands, London, and South) and supporting 150+ Islamic societies. The mission established in the Cadbury Room in 1963, to serve Muslim students with excellence, integrity, and unity, continues with every new generation of ISoc leaders.

Defining Moments

## Chapters that Shaped History

From hosting world leaders to launching landmark campaigns, the moments that defined FOSIS.

01

Nov–Dec 1964

### Hosting Malcolm X in Britain

Just one year after its founding, FOSIS organised the UK speaking tour of El-Hajj Malik El-Shabazz (Malcolm X). Ebrahimsa Mohamed, with Abdul Wahid Hamid, Hoossain Rajah, and Ghayasuddin Siddiqui, coordinated five engagements: Malaysia Hall (22 Nov), a formal FOSIS reception (1 Dec), the Oxford Union (3 Dec), Manchester and Sheffield (4 Dec), and the Islamic Cultural Centre (5 Dec). Malcolm X was assassinated ten weeks later.

02

1970s–present

### Scholars Who Shaped a Generation

For six decades, the FOSIS Annual Winter Gathering has drawn the world’s foremost Islamic scholars to British campuses. Sheikh Ahmed Deedat addressed the 23rd Annual Winter Gathering. Dr Khurram Murad, Imam Siraj Wahhaj, and many others have addressed Muslim students through FOSIS, nurturing a generation of confident, grounded Muslim leaders.

03

1995–1996

### Co-Founding FEMYSO in Leicester

After the 1995 ‘Islam in Europe’ conference in Sweden, FOSIS co-hosts the founding meeting of FEMYSO in Leicester in June 1996. Thirty-five participants from 19 organisations across 11 countries establish what becomes Europe’s foremost Muslim youth platform, now representing 32 member organisations across 22 European countries, headquartered in Brussels.

04

1963 & beyond

### Seeding Muslim Organisations Globally

FOSIS’s founding generation built more than a national body. Ahmad Totonji co-founded the MSA in the USA (January 1963) and helped shape IIFSO (1969). All three MSA founding architects, Totonji, Barzinji, and Talib, were northern Iraqi students who studied engineering in Britain, whose networks and experience in FOSIS directly fed into the global Muslim student movement.

05

2015

### #StudentNotSuspect

FOSIS led the student response to the Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015, organising emergency motions at LSE, UCL, SOAS, Queen Mary’s, Cardiff, and Kingston. The #StudentNotSuspect campaign put the civil liberties of Muslim students at the centre of national debate, FOSIS’s most consequential political campaign in modern history.

06

Ongoing

### Believe & Do Good

FOSIS’s national service campaigns unite Islamic Societies across the country. Hundreds of students provide meals for the homeless, food bank support, blood drives, and gifts for children in hospitals, demonstrating that Muslim student leadership is as much about civic service as it is about campus advocacy. A Mount Snowdon Trek raised £30,000 alone.

The Founders & Pioneers

## People Who Built FOSIS

AT

### Dr Ahmad Totonji

Co-Founder, 1963

Born in northern Iraq, Ahmad Totonji came to Britain as a student and co-founded MSS (1960), UMSO (1961), and FOSIS (1963) in rapid succession. In January 1963, months before FOSIS was formally launched, he was also one of the co-founders of the Muslim Students Association (MSA) of the USA and Canada at the University of Illinois, where he studied for a PhD in Petroleum Engineering. He later served as Deputy Secretary-General of the World Assembly of Muslim Youth (WAMY) from 1973 to 1979, and helped establish the IIFSO (1969) and ISNA, making him one of the most significant architects of global Muslim student movements in the 20th century.

EM

### Ebrahimsa Mohamed

General Secretary 1964 · President 1967–68

South African-born Ebrahimsa Mohamed (1937–2017) was FOSIS General Secretary in 1964, the year he, with Abdul Wahid Hamid, Hoossain Rajah, and Ghayasuddin Siddiqui, organised Malcolm X’s landmark five-city British tour across November and December. Elected President for 1967–68, his most enduring contribution was securing the purchase of 38 Mapesbury Road, Kilburn (1971), which opened as FOSIS’s permanent hostel and head office in November 1972. As warden-in-residence, he made Mapesbury Road a hub for the entire British Muslim community, hosting weddings, conversions, and welcoming generations of new Muslim students to Britain.

ZM

### Zara Mohammed

President 2016–17 · First Female President

After completing a BA in Law and Politics (2013) and an LLM in Human Rights Law (2014), and serving three years on the FOSIS Executive Team, Zara Mohammed was elected FOSIS President in 2016, the first woman to lead the organisation in its 53-year history. She served as MCB Assistant Secretary General from 2018 to 2020, before being elected the youngest and first female Secretary General of the Muslim Council of Britain in January 2021.

A Global Legacy

## Born in Britain, Built for the World

- 1960  Muslim Students Society UK & Eire (MSS) Co-founded by Ahmad Totonji. The first organised attempt to unify Muslim students across Britain and Ireland, and the direct precursor to FOSIS.

- 1963  Muslim Students Association, USA & Canada (MSA) Co-founded at the University of Illinois in January 1963 by Ahmad Totonji and 70 students. Now the largest Muslim student organisation in North America, with chapters at over 600 universities.

- 1969  International Islamic Federation of Student Organizations (IIFSO) Formally established in Aachen, Germany. Rooted in a 1966 initiative at the University of Ibadan, Nigeria. FOSIS’s founding generation, especially Totonji, helped shape this global federation spanning five continents.

- 1996  Forum of European Muslim Youth and Student Organisations (FEMYSO) Co-founded in Leicester by FOSIS and 18 other organisations from 11 countries, following a 1995 preparatory meeting in Sweden. Now 32 member organisations across 22 European countries, headquartered in Brussels.
