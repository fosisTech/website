# IHSAAN 2024: How 3,000 Muslim Graduates Found Their Path

This year’s IHSAAN Careers Conference was the biggest in FOSIS’s history. Here’s what happened, what we learned, and what it means for the future of Muslim graduates in the UK.

On a crisp October morning in Birmingham, more than 3,000 Muslim students and graduates filed into the IHSAAN Careers Conference, the largest gathering of its kind in FOSIS’s 61-year history. By the end of the day, over 400 of them had spoken with graduate employers, 120 had secured interview slots, and dozens had left with job offers in hand.

IHSAAN, which means ‘excellence’ in Arabic, has been FOSIS’s flagship careers programme since 2016. What began as a modest networking event for a few hundred students has grown into one of the most significant Muslim graduate recruitment platforms in the country, attracting employers from law, finance, medicine, technology, and the civil service.

## A Record-Breaking Year

The 2024 conference marked several firsts. For the first time, IHSAAN ran across two days, with the second day dedicated entirely to postgraduate students and those re-entering the workforce. For the first time, a panel on entrepreneurship was oversubscribed within minutes of registration opening. And for the first time, a major international law firm announced a dedicated IHSAAN scholarship for Muslim graduates from low-income backgrounds.

Sixty-two employers attended this year, up from forty-seven in 2023. Among them: KPMG, Linklaters, NHS England, the Home Office, Goldman Sachs, and a growing number of purpose-driven start-ups founded by FOSIS alumni. The message was clear: the Muslim graduate talent pool is not just large, it is exceptional.

## What Employers Are Saying

We spoke to graduate recruitment leads at three attending firms. The consensus? Muslim graduates bring something distinctive to the table. “We see candidates who are deeply purpose-driven, who understand diverse communities, who have often navigated complex social environments,” said one recruitment partner at a leading consultancy. “Those are exactly the qualities we want at senior levels.”

Several employers noted that IHSAAN candidates consistently outperform in structured interviews, a finding that Samir Sidat, FOSIS’s Head of IHSAAN, attributes to the mock interview workshops FOSIS runs in the weeks before the conference. “We spend a lot of time preparing students for the specific demands of corporate recruitment,” he said. “Interview technique is a skill, and it’s one that can be taught.”

## The Barriers Still Standing

Despite the success, IHSAAN 2024 also surfaced the persistent barriers Muslim graduates face. In a post-event survey, 68% of attendees said they had experienced some form of discrimination in a previous application process, ranging from name-based bias to questions about religious practice. Forty-three percent said they had considered changing their name on their CV.

These numbers are not new. But hearing them articulated by 3,000 students in a single room is a different thing altogether. FOSIS President Sohaib Saleem addressed this in his opening remarks: “We are not here because we need permission to succeed. We are here because we choose to, and because our community deserves nothing less than excellence.”

## What Comes Next

IHSAAN is expanding in 2025. FOSIS is piloting a regional IHSAAN series, bringing the careers programme to cities outside London and Birmingham for the first time. A new mentorship platform, connecting current students with FOSIS alumni in their chosen field, is also in development.

The message of IHSAAN 2024 was simple: Muslim graduates are ready. The question for employers is whether they are.
