One year ago, the world witnessed one of the most astonishing collapses of a modern authoritarian regime, one that, only weeks earlier, had appeared secure. The 53-year Assad dynasty, first under **[Hafez Al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafez_al-Assad)** and then his son [Bashar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashar_al-Assad), finally came to an end. For Syrians, this was not a mere political transformation; it marked the close of a long, painful chapter marked by mass imprisonment, torture, disappearances, exile, and the silencing of entire generations. This symbolised a **monumental shift** where a new era of hope began for the Syrian people, and a new source of hope was born for people struggling against oppression across the world.

For Muslims globally, this moment carried particular emotional and spiritual weight. The suffering of our Syrian brothers and sisters echoed across the Ummah, and their perseverance should become a mirror through which we could re-examine our duties, our courage, and our understanding of divine justice. With the fall of the regime came renewed hope for oppressed people across the world who saw in this victory a reminder that tyranny is never absolute and that Allah is always in control.

If we celebrate without reflecting and extracting lessons, then we risk reducing a profound historical event to a fleeting moment of emotion. We have a responsibility to engage critically with events, to connect them to our divine sources, and to translate them into meaningful action.

---

## **Prophetic Resistance Through Patience**

When recounting the life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, our focus oft gravitates toward the moments of triumph: the victory at Badr, the miraculous defence during the Battle of the Trench, or the opening of Makkah. These are indeed monumental victories in Islamic history where the promise of Allah materialised in clear, visible form.

These victories, however, **cannot be seen as isolated moments**. The foundations of the Prophet’s mission were laid in struggle, sacrifice, and steadfastness long before success appeared on the horizon. For 13 years in Makkah, Muslims endured unimaginable persecution. The Prophet ﷺ was mocked, boycotted, physically attacked, and nearly assassinated. His companions were tortured, beaten, and martyred. Families were torn apart. Entire clans were subjected to starvation.

The stories of [Sumayyah](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/sumayyah-the-first-martyr) and [Yasir](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/watch/series/ammar-ibn-yasir-a-legacy-of-sacrifice-the-firsts) (RA), the first martyrs of Islam, embody this reality. They were an elderly, poor couple with no political power and no tribal protection. Under torture, they refused to denounce their faith, until they returned to their Lord with honour. Their son, Ammar (RA), would himself undergo immense persecution but was reassured by Allah and His Messenger that his faith remained intact.

These stories must be taken as both lessons from history and a means to fortify our spirit. They teach us that victory is not defined by the immediate circumstances of power, safety, or comfort. Instead, they show that patience is not passive endurance but an active, principled commitment to truth especially when the world tries to beat that truth out of you.

Allah says in the Qur’an (2:214):

*Do you think you will enter Paradise without being tested like those before you? They were afflicted with suffering and hardship and were so severely shaken that even the Messenger and the believers with him cried out, ‘When will the help of Allah come?’ Truly, the help of Allah is near.*

How difficult must it have been for the Muslims at the time to see their victory? How difficult must it have been for the Algerians fighting their French colonisers to see their liberation? How difficult must it be for our Palestinian brothers and sisters today, being ethnically cleansed and genocided, to see their freedom? And yet, Allah reminds us: *His victory is near.* Not on our timetable, not according to worldly calculations, but by His perfect wisdom.

The fall of the Assad regime is a contemporary reminder of this timeless truth: even the most entrenched tyranny collapses when Allah wills.

## **Patience Through Action**

Patience, often misunderstood as waiting passively, is never synonymous with inaction. **The Syrian people did not survive five decades of subjugation through silence or resignation**. Their patience manifested through persistence, community solidarity, preservation of dignity, quiet acts of defiance, and eventually, through courageous uprising.

True patience is marked by movement. It is the steady refusal to accept injustice as normal. It is choosing to act even when victory seems impossible. It is the conviction that Allah sees our efforts and multiplies them, even if the world appears unchanged.

The Seerah offers countless examples of patience intertwined with struggle. The Muslims in Makkah secretly met in Dar al-Arqam to learn the Qur’an. They continued calling to Allah despite social ostracisation. They migrated to Abyssinia to preserve their faith. They strategised, adapted, and endured.

Likewise, Syrians resisted through documentation of crimes, underground education, community networks, peaceful protest, intellectual resistance, and eventually armed themselves when all else failed. Their perseverance teaches us that patience does not mean accepting injustice, it means continuing to resist it with integrity, courage, and reliance on Allah.

As students, we must internalise the principles in this active form of patience. Whether the arena is political organising, raising awareness, charity work, academic excellence, or resisting hate on campus, our efforts must be consistent and intentional. Your patience has little value if all it amounts to is stagnation and intentional inaction.

## **The Role of Students**

Students have always played a transformative role in the Muslim Ummah. The companions who first carried Islam to Abyssinia, who memorised Qur’an, who debated Quraysh, and who spread the message across Arabia – many were young. History repeats this pattern: from anti-colonial movements across the Muslim world to global protests for justice today, students have been the first to speak, the quickest to mobilise, and the hardest to silence.

The fall of the Assad regime again illuminates how essential intellectual courage, moral clarity, and organised resistance are; qualities we must always seek to embody as students. We are always expected to ask the difficult questions, to seek truth even when it is inconvenient, and to challenge structures of oppression both globally and within our own societies.

Students today have access to information, digital platforms, academic institutions, organisational opportunities that earlier generations could only dream of. But these tools must be guided by Islamic ethics. Knowledge without character breeds arrogance. Activism without spirituality becomes hollow.

From the lessons of Syria, we as students must:

- Strengthen our connection to the Qur’an and Sunnah to ground their activism.

- Educate ourselves on global injustices, especially those affecting the Ummah.

- Speak truth, even when unpopular.

- Support oppressed peoples materially and morally.

- Build communities of resistance on campus.

- Engage in disciplined, informed activism.

- Prepare ourselves intellectually and spiritually for future leadership.

The Syrian victory teaches us that regimes fall not only from physical struggle, but from decades of intellectual, moral, and communal resilience. We should always seek to be the forerunners of that resilience.

---

## **Conclusion**

The fall of the Assad regime stands as a powerful reminder that tyranny is never permanent and that Allah’s promise is always true. But it also places a responsibility on us as Muslim students to learn, reflect, and act.

We must embrace prophetic patience, rooted not in passivity but in purposeful struggle. We must adopt a long-term vision of justice that does not crumble during hardship. And we must take inspiration from Syrians who preserved their dignity under enormous pressure.

History is not meant to be observed from a distance. It is meant to shape us.

May Allah grant victory to the oppressed, wisdom to the students who seek truth, and sincerity to all who work for justice, may He shower his mercy and blessings upon all those who struggle for His sake, and may those who suffer at the hands of oppressors around the world receive their justice, in this world and the next.

Ameen.
